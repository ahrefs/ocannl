Module Bench_design

Balanced timing-position designs for interleaved benchmark arms (gh-ocannl-743).

A harness that times n arms against each other visits them in batches: every batch times every arm once, in some order, and the order is the design. Two position effects confound an arm with where it was visited. A LINEAR one (monotone session drift: clocks settling, a queue filling) is cancelled by mirroring: each batch is followed by its exact reverse, so every pair of arms is visited in both orders equally often and every arm's mean position is the same. A NONLINEAR one (a first-launch warm-up, the edges of a round) is not -- an arm visited at the edges more often than another still carries more of it. That needs every arm's position HISTOGRAM to be the same, which mirroring alone does not give.

The design here is a mirrored rotation whose per-pair offsets are spread over the arm count rather than advanced by one: pair p of P = batches / 2 rotates the arm order by 2 * floor (p * (n / 2) / P) for an even n (the even offsets, spread), by floor (p * n / P) for an odd one. Its properties, each pinned by test/operations/bench_design:

  • every pair of arms is exchanged equally often, at every even batch count (the mirror);
  • the position histograms are EQUAL across arms as soon as n divides batches for an even n, and 2n does for an odd one. That is the least batch count any design can do it at: each position holds batches visits to share among n arms, and mirrored pairs make batches even. The rotation advanced by one per pair, which projection_shape_bench used before, needs 2n for every n;
  • below that, for an even n, no arm visits the same position CLASS (a position and its mirror, q and n - 1 - q) twice before batches reaches n: the even offsets keep each arm's visits on one parity, and a position and its mirror have opposite parities. So an edge position falls to an arm at most once per mirrored pair, where the advanced-by-one rotation hands the first arm both edges in its first two pairs.

Below the exact count no design balances every histogram, so describe says which regime a run is in and the least batch count that would balance it. A seeded random order -- the other design the issue named -- balances only in expectation, never exactly, and would need its generator kept in step across the two languages that share this design; it was not taken.

The design is shared with the process-level driver benchmarks/bin_cells.py, which visits whole bench processes as arms in the same order; that driver's design_orders is held equal to orders over the grid test/operations/bench_design prints.
