Module Test_scan.Policy_invariance_scan

Which tests the numerics-mode matrix runs, derived from the policy their modules name (gh-ocannl-1104).

The @policy-invariance family (gh-ocannl-1104): which tests the daily sweep's policy units run under every non-default fp16/bf16 mode, derived from what the tests' own sources name.

A member is a TEST -- a (test) or (tests) program, an inline-test library, or an (executable) some rule runs on a runtest- alias, directly or by writing a target a golden-diff rule on one checks (runs_as_test) -- one of whose own modules names the numerics policy: the module Ir.Numerics itself, or a value of test/support/test_utils.ml whose definition does -- its with_policy wrappers, derived by wrappers rather than listed. Those are the programs that set the process-wide policy for some of their legs and read the ambient one in others, which is what makes a default-labelled claim in them test another policy whenever the caller exported a mode (OCANNL_FP16_ARITHMETIC, OCANNL_BF16_ARITHMETIC), with no run but the sweep's ever exporting one.

What it reads, and deliberately does not:

  • a member's own modules, and every arm of a (select …) that produces one, under the scanner's lexical module model (Config_key_scan.module_reference_sites): an alias or an open of Ir counts, a comment or a string literal spelling Numerics does not;
  • of test/support, test_utils.ml alone. A support value that sets the policy and restores it inside its own computation -- Ll_test.tensorized_capability's tf32 lift, which runs no code of its caller's under it -- makes no member of its callers;
  • no program that is not run as a test: a benchmark under bin/ naming the policy measures under whatever mode it is given, which is its caller's choice rather than a claim;
  • of the family stanza, its direct (alias …) entries, each with the stanza's (enabled_if …); what a listed member's own dependencies pull in is that member's to answer for;
  • nothing about the precisions a test computes at. A test computing in half without naming the policy is outside the family: naming it is the decision that makes a test's legs depend on the mode, and the remaining tests answer for the default mode in every other run.

A member whose claims deliberately follow the DEFAULT policy -- a premise it states as a claim -- opts out inside its own stanza, the reason saying which claims and why:

; ocannl-policy-invariance: exempt -- <reason>

and the alias leaves it out. The opt-out is the whole stanza under every mode: a member that holds under some modes and not others pins the legs that need the default instead (Test_utils.with_policy), which keeps the rest of it in the matrix.
